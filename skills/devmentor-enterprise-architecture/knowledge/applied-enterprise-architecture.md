# Applied Enterprise Architecture

**Domain:** 12 - Enterprise and Business Architecture
**Primary sources:** A Holistic View of Software and Enterprise Architecture (Carter, 2024); Enterprise Architecture With .NET (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024); Enterprise Fortress: The Ultimate Handbook for Enterprise Security Architecture (Stevens, 2025); Unit-Oriented Enterprise Architecture (Milchman, 2025)

## Overview
Applied enterprise architecture is the practice of taking EA out of the framework binder and making it work in a real organization: aligning technology with business goals, decomposing large information systems into governable parts, building systems that are secure and resilient by design, and giving stakeholders a shared, comprehensible view of how everything fits together. The four sources here approach the same problem from complementary angles: a holistic, diagram-first view spanning the SDLC and the major EA frameworks (Carter); a hands-on, principles-driven blueprint for industrializing an information system, demonstrated in .NET (Gouigoux & Tamzalit); a security-architecture handbook for building the "enterprise fortress" with governance and zero trust (Stevens); and a unit-oriented theory for constructing very large sociotechnical systems out of uniform building blocks (Milchman).

Why it matters in the SDLC: most software is built inside an enterprise that already has processes, data, organizational structure, regulatory obligations, and a security posture. A technically excellent system that is misaligned with the business, duplicates master data, cannot be governed, or is insecure is a failed system. Applied EA supplies the connective tissue, the frameworks, the business-capability maps, the security architecture, and the decomposition principles, so that what gets built corresponds to how the enterprise actually operates and can survive contact with reality.

## Core concepts

**The enterprise architect's role.** EAs align technology initiatives with business goals, facilitate organizational change, ensure regulatory compliance, and steward EA frameworks (e.g. TOGAF). They produce comprehensive designs and evaluate/recommend technology against the organization's architectural principles and standards, fostering agility, innovation, and cost-effectiveness (Carter, 2024).

**Holistic diagramming.** A deliberately generic, abstraction-first diagramming approach that represents servers, containers, storage, processing, and transport with simple uniform shapes (boxes for servers, lines for transport, triangles for APIs) so the same patterns apply across monoliths, microservices, and data mesh. The diagram serves as an index to deeper documentation: a database shape signals the need for ERDs/schema docs, transport lines signal contract diagrams, servers signal deployment diagrams, and API triangles signal API specifications. Each specialist team can locate the part of the bigger picture it owns (Carter, 2024).

**Business alignment and the CIGREF map (urbanization).** A three-layer model: a *process layer* (objectives, what the organization does), a *functional layer* or business capability map (BCM, how those objectives are realized, who can do what), and a *software layer*. The often-forgotten middle BCM is where misalignment originates ("a vision without a plan is just a wish"). The functional layer is organized using the city metaphor of *urbanization*: **zones/areas** (large groupings like a city's commercial/industrial/living areas), **quarters/neighborhoods** (finer local organization), and **blocks/islands** (fine divisions where closely related functions share a tool or team). A well-organized enterprise typically has five zones: master data management, shared tools, externally oriented (collaborative) functions, governance/piloting, and business-oriented functions (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).

**Industrialization of software: modularization + standardization.** The two roots of industrialization are decomposing systems into smaller ones (modularization) and joining modules with standard interfaces (standardization). Software is still largely craftsmanship; industrialization normalizes the process so that quality no longer depends only on a few highly skilled individuals, bringing predictability, quality, and time-to-market. Craftsmanship is the step before industrialization, not its enemy (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).

**Technical debt as a metaphor.** Cutting corners to buy time incurs "interest" paid as ongoing maintenance for as long as the low-quality module stays active; the lower the quality, the higher the interest rate (time spent keeping it running instead of delivering value). At the extreme, all capacity is spent paying interest (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).

**Security architecture and the CIA triad.** Enterprise security architecture protects information through the bedrock model of Confidentiality (access control, encryption), Integrity (checksums, hashing, digital signatures), and Availability (DoS protection, redundancy). Security is a business enabler aligned to organizational goals, not a bolt-on (Stevens, 2025).

**Sociotechnical units and large sociotechnical systems (LSS).** A unit-oriented view treats the enterprise as a collection of *uniformly constructed sociotechnical units*, each integrating social, physical, and digital elements and designed to function both independently and synergistically. It deliberately concentrates on the architectural essence (components and their linkages) rather than on strategy/governance/resource-optimization concerns. Units compose into an LSS; the recurring metaphor is an enterprise as an armada of fleets and flotillas with an integrative core (central control/strategy) and an operational periphery (agile, locally connected units), balancing central control against local autonomy (Milchman, 2025).

## Frameworks & models

**TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework).** A comprehensive, iterative methodology centered on the Architecture Development Method (ADM): Preliminary, A (Architecture Vision), B (Business Architecture), C (Information Systems Architectures: data + application), D (Technology Architecture), E (Opportunities and Solutions), F (Migration Planning), G (Implementation Governance), H (Architecture Change Management), plus continuous Requirements Management. Widely adopted in finance, healthcare, and government to align IT with business strategy. Strength: structured and flexible; weakness: heavy and complex (Carter, 2024; also referenced as an EA-and-security framework by Stevens, 2025).

**Zachman Framework.** A logical schema (a 6x6 matrix) for organizing and classifying descriptive representations of an enterprise from multiple perspectives, giving a comprehensive, systematic view of the whole organization (Carter, 2024).

**FEAF (Federal Enterprise Architecture Framework).** A US-government framework built on reference models, guidelines, and standards to drive consistency and interoperability across federal agencies, operating on core alignment principles (Carter, 2024).

**Security frameworks (Stevens, 2025):**
- **SABSA (Sherwood Applied Business Security Architecture)** - a security-focused, business-driven EA framework. Core is the SABSA Matrix plus Business Attribute Profiling; risk-driven and tightly aligned to business needs. Strength: comprehensive and risk-tailored; weakness: complex to apply.
- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework** - widely used function-based framework (Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover).
- **ISO/IEC 27001** - certifiable information-security management system standard.
- **COBIT** - governance-and-management framework for enterprise IT.
- A comparison-and-selection discipline (matching framework to organization context) is itself part of the practice.

**Unit-oriented compositional patterns (Milchman, 2025).** Four ways to compose units into an LSS, each with explicit trade-offs (see Patterns).

## Patterns

- **Holistic diagram as documentation index** - Intent: use a single abstraction-first diagram with uniform shapes to give every stakeholder a shared system view and to drive which detailed artifacts are needed. When to use: communicating architecture across mixed-skill audiences and planning documentation. Trade-offs: clarity and shared understanding vs. deliberate loss of implementation detail (must be backed by deeper docs) (Carter, 2024).
- **Urbanization (zones / quarters / blocks)** - Intent: organize the business-capability map like a city to keep functions cohesive and aligned with the org chart. When to use: structuring the functional layer of an information system; diagnosing misalignment. Trade-offs: durable, business-aligned structure vs. requiring a BCM that most companies lack and must first build (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).
- **Externalization of business rules / processes / authorization** - Intent: keep processes, business rules, and authorization out of application code so strategy changes do not force software changes. When to use: industrializing an information system for agility. Trade-offs: business agility and reuse vs. additional infrastructure (rules engines, process/low-code platforms, externalized identity) (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).
- **Write-only data referential (then add query)** - Intent: build master-data services with a clear write side, then add query capabilities, governing important data (customers, products) in a dedicated MDM zone. When to use: eliminating duplicated, divergent master data across the estate. Trade-offs: single source of truth and clear governance vs. up-front service design and migration effort (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).
- **Defense in depth (layered security)** - Intent: never rely on a single control; stack firewalls, IDS/IPS, encryption, and access controls so a failure in one layer is backed by others. When to use: any enterprise security architecture. Trade-offs: strong resilience to single-control failure vs. cost and operational complexity of multiple layers (Stevens, 2025).
- **Zero trust architecture** - Intent: assume no implicit trust; verify every access continuously with least privilege and network segmentation. When to use: modern distributed/cloud enterprises where the perimeter has dissolved. Trade-offs: greatly reduced lateral-movement risk vs. implementation complexity and segmentation challenges (Stevens, 2025).
- **Security by design / minimize attack surface** - Intent: build security into the system from the ground up and remove unnecessary services, protocols, and applications. When to use: every system from inception. Trade-offs: far cheaper and stronger than bolt-on security vs. requiring security thinking at every SDLC stage (Stevens, 2025).
- **Distributed composition** - Intent: decentralize components so they operate independently. When to use: maximizing resilience, scalability, and flexibility across locations/nodes. Trade-offs: redundancy and localized-failure tolerance vs. coordination/synchronization overhead and risk of divergent standards (Milchman, 2025).
- **Partitioned composition** - Intent: divide the system into distinct, non-overlapping sections with clear responsibilities. When to use: simplifying management and isolating functions/failures. Trade-offs: clarity, specialization, and contained failures vs. effort duplication and cross-partition coordination/silos (Milchman, 2025).
- **Interlocked composition** - Intent: tightly link components so they share resources and coordinate closely for optimized performance. When to use: when seamless information/resource flow across parts is paramount. Trade-offs: high efficiency and integration vs. interdependency, harder troubleshooting, and systemic vulnerability (one failure can cascade) (Milchman, 2025).
- **Fused composition** - Intent: merge components into a unified whole. When to use: where the strongest cohesion is required. Trade-offs: maximal integration vs. loss of independent evolvability (Milchman, 2025).
- **Integrative core + operational periphery (armada/fleet/flotilla)** - Intent: keep a central integrative core for strategy and governance while letting operational units stay agile and locally connected, with optional double-nesting for very large enterprises. When to use: globally distributed enterprises balancing coherence against local responsiveness. Trade-offs: unified direction plus local autonomy vs. nesting complexity that must be hidden behind a flat-network appearance (Milchman, 2025).

## Principles & best practices
- Treat the information system as a model of the business system; keep the business-capability map (the forgotten middle layer) explicit and aligned to the org chart (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).
- Pursue industrialization through modularization plus standardization; aim for quality that does not depend solely on heroic individuals (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).
- Govern master data in a dedicated zone with clear ownership to prevent costly, divergent duplication (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).
- Make security a business-aligned design concern from the start; apply least privilege, separation of duties, fail-safe defaults, and minimal attack surface (Stevens, 2025).
- Choose a security framework deliberately by matching it to organizational context rather than defaulting to the best-known one (Stevens, 2025).
- Communicate architecture with abstraction-first holistic diagrams that index, rather than replace, detailed documentation (Carter, 2024).
- For very large systems, build from uniform sociotechnical units and choose a compositional pattern consciously per area (Milchman, 2025).
- Balance central control with local autonomy: strong integrative core, agile operational periphery (Milchman, 2025).

## Anti-patterns & pitfalls
- **Pure technical integration** - implementing a process directly in software instead of the process layer, so every strategy or rule change forces a software change ("we are dragged down by IT") (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).
- **Applicative silos** - two parts of the organization building IT without talking, producing duplicated, incompatible data and later painful, insecure point-to-point links (a GDPR/confidentiality risk) (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).
- **Monolith that absorbs shared functions** - an application that re-implements functions that should be shared, multiplying duplicated data with no clear source of truth (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).
- **Functional multiple implementations** - the same function (e.g. a budget summary, a readership count) computed differently in different applications, yielding incompatible numbers and bad decisions (Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).
- **Bolt-on security** - adding security after the system is built rather than designing it in, leaving a large attack surface (Stevens, 2025).
- **Single-layer defense** - relying on one control with no defense in depth, so one failure is total (Stevens, 2025).
- **Interlocked/fused everywhere** - over-coupling units for short-term efficiency, creating cascading-failure risk and killing independent evolvability (Milchman, 2025).
- **Ivory-tower framework worship** - adopting TOGAF/Zachman ceremonially without aligning to real business capabilities and operations (cross-cutting caution across Carter, 2024 and Gouigoux & Tamzalit, 2024).

## Decision guidance
- **Pick the framework for the job.** TOGAF for end-to-end methodology and migration governance; Zachman for classification/perspective completeness; FEAF for federated public-sector interoperability; SABSA/NIST/ISO 27001/COBIT for the security and governance dimension. Match to context; do not default to the most famous.
- **Always surface the business-capability map.** When diagnosing misalignment, look for the missing middle layer first; structure it via urbanization zones and check that quarters map to organizational directions.
- **Industrialize through modular, standardized services**, externalizing processes, rules, and authorization, and centralizing master data, to gain agility and eliminate duplication.
- **Design security in from inception** using the CIA triad, defense in depth, least privilege, and (for distributed/cloud estates) zero trust; choose the framework after assessing risk and context.
- **For large systems, compose deliberately.** Favor distributed/partitioned composition where resilience and clear ownership matter; reserve interlocked/fused composition for areas that genuinely require tight coordination, and keep an integrative core thin enough to preserve operational agility.
- **Communicate with holistic diagrams** that give a shared picture and point to the detailed artifacts each discipline needs.

## Recent developments (2024-2026)

- **AI-augmented applied EA tooling.** Through 2025, the applied work captured here (holistic diagramming, business-capability mapping, urbanization of the functional layer, master-data governance) is increasingly produced and validated with agentic AI in EA suites that automate data validation, capability mapping, and artifact creation, and generate standards-compliant diagrams (e.g. ArchiMate) from prompts. (ACL Digital, 2025: https://www.acldigital.com/blogs/how-ai-augmented-enterprise-architecture-is-redefining-business-agility-in-2025-and-beyond ; Visual Paradigm: https://blog.visual-paradigm.com/from-strategy-to-execution-accelerating-enterprise-architecture-with-ai-powered-archimate-modeling/)
- **Security architecture: AI as both target and tool.** Zero trust, defense in depth, and security-by-design remain the bedrock, but 2025 practice extends them to securing AI agents and AI-ready data and to using AI for detection and validation; the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (now CSF 2.0, with the added Govern function) is the current reference behind the Identify/Protect/Detect/Respond/Recover model in the source. (Gartner AI implementation, 2025: https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/ai-implementation)
- **TOGAF 10 maintained and bundled with companion standards.** The framework anchoring much of this file received a 2025 maintenance update and was placed in The Open Group's Portfolio of Digital Open Standards alongside ArchiMate, IT4IT, Open Agile Architecture, and the Digital Practitioner Body of Knowledge. (The Open Group Blog, July 2025: https://blog.opengroup.org/2025/07/01/navigating-the-togaf-standard-10th-edition-updates-and-insights/)
- **AI agents in enterprise applications raise the stakes for industrialization.** Gartner's forecast that 40% of enterprise apps will embed task-specific AI agents by end of 2026 (from under 5% in 2025) makes the source's industrialization themes (modularization, standardization, externalized rules/process/authorization, single-source master data) more important, since agents need governed, well-isolated capabilities and trustworthy data to act on. (Gartner, Aug 2025: https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-08-26-gartner-predicts-40-percent-of-enterprise-apps-will-feature-task-specific-ai-agents-by-2026-up-from-less-than-5-percent-in-2025)
- **Frameworks and compositional theory stable.** TOGAF, Zachman, FEAF, SABSA/NIST/ISO 27001/COBIT, the CIGREF urbanization model, and unit-oriented composition remain current; the 2024-2026 change is AI-assisted production of artifacts and the added concern of securing and governing AI within the estate.
