# Futures Thinking

**Domain:** 03 - Product Discovery and Planning
**Primary sources:** Making Futures Work: Integrating Futures Thinking for Design, Innovation, and Strategy (Phil Balagtas, 2024, O'Reilly)

## Overview
Making Futures Work is a practical handbook for bringing Futures Thinking (also called strategic foresight) into design, innovation, and product strategy. Balagtas, founder of the Design Futures Initiative and the global Speculative Futures network, demystifies a field with roots at the RAND Corporation in the 1950s into a process designers and strategists can actually run inside organizations. The premise: we cannot contact the future, so we anticipate it by reading patterns in the present and recent past, then explore multiple alternative futures rather than predicting one.

The book is explicitly about *not predicting*. Foresight uses multiple scenarios to widen peripheral vision and prepare for change, and then design is used to move from anticipating what *will* happen to shaping what *should* happen. For product work, this complements roadmapping and discovery: it future-proofs decisions, surfaces threats and opportunities on the periphery, and grounds a long-term North Star in real signals rather than wishful extrapolation.

## Core concepts

- **Strategic Foresight.** The core discipline for gathering and processing information about future operating environments - trends and developments across political, economic, social, technological, and legal dimensions - and projecting them forward to develop perspectives on how the future could unfold.
- **Signals.** Early, concrete indicators of change on the periphery (a new product, behavior, policy, technology) that you interpret and generalize from.
- **Trends (by scale).** Megatrends (largest, longest), macrotrends, microtrends, and fads (shortest, shallowest). Distinguishing them governs how much weight to give each in a worldview.
- **The Futures Cone (Joseph Voros, 2003).** A cone of possibilities expanding over time: the **Projected/Probable** future at the center (business as usual - which inherits today's problems), then **Plausible** (could happen), **Possible** (might happen), and **Preposterous** (cannot happen given current limits - e.g., teleportation). Not strictly a method but a principle: the future is plural, and we have agency to imagine many scenarios.
- **Backcasting.** Working backward from a desired future vision (the North Star) to identify the critical steps, milestones, and goals needed to reach it - a roadmap built from the future toward today.
- **Speculative / Critical Design and Design Fiction.** Design used to communicate, provoke, and critique possible futures (artifacts from a potential future that provoke discussion), distinct from designing a single committed product.

## Frameworks & models

**The process stages (Balagtas' structure).**
- **Stage 0 - Preparing for futures work.** Understand the audience/organization (situational awareness), instill a futures mindset (examples, embracing fear of failure, a premortem mindset), frame the problem (focal issue, time horizon, geographic and demographic boundaries, complexities), and frame the project (team, timeline, deliverables, funding, tools, repositories).
- **Stage 1 - Strategic Foresight.** Gather trends and futures intelligence; analyze and prioritize the mountain of data (qualitative and quantitative) to converge; build scenarios and map implications.
- **Stage 2 - Designing Futures.** Choose a futures-design format (Speculative Design, Critical Design, Design Fiction, Science Fiction Prototyping, Experiential Futures); design and facilitate an ideation workshop that transports people into a future world and generates novel, provocative concepts.
- **Stage 3 - Designing Strategy.** Tie everything into an actionable plan: plan strategic meetings, set metrics, build a roadmap toward a North Star using backcasting, segment into short- and mid-term stages, and measure success (quantitative and qualitative indicators). Sustain a futures culture beyond the project.

**STEEP.** A multivariate research lens - Social, Technological, Environmental, Economic, Political - for taking a "slice" of a system at a point in time (today, or a future year like 2030) and comparing across times or geographies to uncover conditions, influences, and interdependencies. Always define the time horizon. Feeds other methods (e.g., the Futures Cone).

**Causal Layered Analysis (CLA, Sohail Inayatullah).** Four layers from surface to depth: **Litany** (accepted headlines/"what we accept to be true") → **Systemic causes** → **Worldview** → **Myth/Metaphor**. You move down the left side to uncover deeper causes, then back up the right side to a *transformed* litany. Reveals how culture and systems shape opinions; deeper and slower than other methods, requiring vocabulary fluency - use cautiously.

**Bill Sharpe's Three Horizons.** Three perspectives on change over time, framed as conversations: **H1** (the voice of concern - how we do things today; the current system that will eventually become obsolete), **H2** (the voice of enterprise/entrepreneurship - innovations that bridge H1 and H3, e.g., hybrid cars), **H3** (the voice of aspiration - the transformed future built on changing values, e.g., fully renewable transport). Useful for contextualizing trends, spotting where to innovate/disrupt/intervene, and influencing organizational conversations.

**Jim Dator's four scenario archetypes (1979).** Continuation (business as usual - unrealistic but worth modeling because it inherits today's problems), Collapse (the dark, non-preferable, worst-case future - prepares you for failure), Transformation (the preferred "happy path" of growth and innovation, not necessarily a utopia), and Disciplined Society (society adapts to disruption and finds a new equilibrium - e.g., the COVID-19 response). Provides a baseline set of themes; labels can be softened to "things go well / don't go well" for some audiences.

**The Futures Wheel (implication mapping).** A diagram of consequences/impacts radiating outward from a central event, trend, or idea (first-order, then second- and third-order implications). Also called an implications diagram in corporate settings. One of the most useful and versatile methods for building scenarios and informing future visions.

**The Futures Triangle.** A frame for the influencing forces shaping the future (pull of the future, push of the present, weight of history).

## Patterns
- **Futures Cone framing** - intent: shift people from a single linear "business as usual" future to a plural set of possible/plausible/probable/preferable futures. When to use: introducing foresight to a skeptical audience. Trade-offs: a principle, not a deliverable; needs pairing with concrete methods.
- **STEEP scan** - intent: systematically scan a system across five dimensions at a defined horizon. When to use: trend gathering and environment analysis. Trade-offs: can produce overwhelming data; requires prioritization and a fixed time horizon.
- **Three Horizons** - intent: locate trends and technologies on a current/transition/aspirational map to find intervention points. When to use: portfolio and innovation conversations, managing change resistance. Trade-offs: no "right" horizon; value is in facilitating the conversation.
- **Dator scenario archetypes** - intent: generate a balanced set of alternative futures (continuation, collapse, transformation, discipline) so no single outlook dominates. When to use: scenario planning workshops. Trade-offs: archetypes can feel extreme; reframe labels for the audience.
- **Futures Wheel** - intent: map first-, second-, and third-order implications of a change to build scenarios and surface unintended consequences. When to use: exploring the ripple effects of a trend or decision. Trade-offs: can sprawl; needs facilitation to stay focused.
- **Backcasting** - intent: build a roadmap from a desired future vision back to present-day milestones. When to use: turning a North Star into an actionable plan. Trade-offs: depends on a clear, agreed vision; combine with the Futures Cone to test variant visions.
- **Causal Layered Analysis** - intent: dig beneath surface narratives to systemic causes, worldviews, and myths, then transform them. When to use: deep, high-stakes reframing. Trade-offs: lengthy; needs vocabulary fluency and time.
- **Speculative Design / Design Fiction** - intent: make a future tangible and provocative to spark discussion and critique. When to use: communicating and stress-testing visions. Trade-offs: provocation can be mistaken for prediction; manage expectations.

## Principles & best practices
- Do not predict; explore multiple alternatives and prepare. Futures is not a precise science - being wrong is acceptable and expected.
- Anchor speculation in present signals and trends; you have no channel to the future except the patterns of today and the past.
- Always define the focal issue, time horizon, geography, and demographics before scanning (Stage 0 framing) to avoid drowning in possibilities.
- Use analogies (uncharted island, Mt. Kilimanjaro, theme-park trip, or a one-year product roadmap for change-averse software teams) to make the abstract process relatable.
- Embrace fears and failure explicitly - surface organizational fear (of failure, competition, the CEO, losing funding) in the diagnostic and quell it; consider a premortem.
- Move from anticipation to agency: use design to create the realities you want, not just to react to predicted events.
- Measure futures work with both quantitative and qualitative indicators, and segment the roadmap into short- and mid-term stages so near-term goals connect to the long-term vision.
- Sustain a futures culture beyond any single project through education, tools, repositories, and collaboration across organizational levels.

## Anti-patterns & pitfalls
- **Treating "business as usual" as safe.** The continuation/projected future inherits today's problems and fosters no innovation; modeling it is useful, betting on it is risky.
- **Predicting a single future.** Collapsing the cone to one line removes agency and peripheral vision.
- **Skipping framing (Stage 0).** Generating endless possibilities with no focal issue, horizon, or boundaries causes confusion and anxiety.
- **Mistaking speculative artifacts for forecasts.** Design fiction provokes; it does not predict.
- **Ignoring organizational fear and myopia.** Comfort with iterating on past success ("more SKUs without innovation") blocks long-term thinking.
- **Overusing deep methods carelessly.** CLA and similar are time-intensive and vocabulary-heavy; deploy them deliberately.

## Decision guidance
- Use Futures Thinking to complement (not replace) discovery and roadmapping when the question is long-range, the environment is shifting, or the team is stuck iterating on the present.
- Start with framing (Stage 0): set focal issue, time horizon, geography, demographics, and audience readiness.
- For scanning, run STEEP and sort trends by scale (mega/macro/micro/fad); prioritize before converging on scenarios.
- For scenarios, use Dator's four archetypes for breadth and the Futures Wheel for implications; use the Futures Cone to position them.
- Use Three Horizons to find where to intervene and to manage change resistance; use CLA when a deep reframe is warranted and time allows.
- Convert the chosen preferred future into a plan with backcasting toward a North Star, segment into short/mid-term milestones, and define quantitative and qualitative success indicators.
- Pick the futures-design format (Speculative Design, Design Fiction, Experiential Futures) by intent: communicate, critique, or immerse - and manage the expectation that these provoke rather than predict.

## Recent developments (2024–2026)

- **AI has entered mainstream foresight practice.** A 2025 OECD/World Economic Forum survey of 167 foresight experts across 55 countries found roughly two-thirds already use AI in some part of their foresight work. The most cited benefit (39%) was time efficiency from automating horizon scanning and synthesis, followed by data processing/analysis (17%) and scenario generation (10%). AI accelerates the labor-intensive scanning and signal-clustering that this book describes (STEEP scans, trend gathering) so practitioners can spend more time on interpretation and narrative. (World Economic Forum, 2025; "AI in Strategic Foresight: Reshaping Anticipatory Governance," WEF/OECD, 2025)

- **The human role is reframed, not removed.** The consistent 2025-2026 message from WEF, BCG, and frog/Capgemini is that AI can map the past and model probabilities, but choosing among futures and imagining genuine alternatives remains human work. The framing matches the book's core stance (foresight is not prediction): AI is positioned as a tool for asking better questions, not generating the "right" future. (frog/Capgemini Invent, "Reframing Strategic Foresight in the Age of AI"; BCG, "Navigating the Future with Strategic Foresight," 2025)

- **Foresight repositioned as a strategic must-have amid volatility.** WEF and BCG argue (2025) that compounding disruption (AI, geopolitics, climate) has moved strategic foresight from "nice-to-have" to an essential capability, with foresight-capable organizations shown to be more agile in crises and more consistent on long-term commitments. This raises the stakes on the book's Stage 3 (Designing Strategy) work of connecting scenarios to action. (WEF, "Why strategic foresight prepares organizations for the future," 2025)

- **Experiential and time-efficient formats are gaining traction.** New participatory methods such as "FutureSlam" (combining scenario work, AI-supported reflection, and improvisational performance) and the Bavarian Foresight-Institute's "Scenario Game" aim to let teams experience futures rather than only analyze them, and to compress scenario planning into engaging, time-boxed sessions. These extend the book's experiential-futures and workshop facilitation guidance. (WEF, "Why organizations need to experience the future," 2026; WEF, "A new approach to scenario planning," 2025)

- **Core methods stable.** The Futures Cone, STEEP, Three Horizons, Dator's archetypes, the Futures Wheel, CLA, and backcasting remain the standard toolkit. The 2024-2026 shift is operational (AI-assisted scanning and scenario drafting) and positional (foresight elevated in strategy), not a change to the underlying frameworks.
