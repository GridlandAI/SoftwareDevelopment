# LLM Design Patterns

**Domain:** 10 - AI, GenAI, and Agentic Systems
**Primary sources:** *LLM Design Patterns: A Practical Guide to Building Robust LLM Applications* (Huang, 2025)

## Overview
Large language models are powerful but unreliable building blocks: they hallucinate, are nondeterministic, have a fixed knowledge cutoff, and can be manipulated. *LLM Design Patterns* catalogs reusable solutions for the full lifecycle of building robust LLM applications, organized into five arcs: data preparation, training/optimization, evaluation/interpretation, advanced prompting, and retrieval/knowledge integration culminating in agentic systems. For a software-development knowledge base, the load-bearing patterns sit in the last three arcs: prompting strategies that elicit reasoning, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and its advanced variants, evaluation (including LLM-as-judge), model-efficiency techniques (fine-tuning, pruning, quantization, caching), safety/robustness (adversarial attacks, prompt injection, guardrails), and agentic orchestration.

The unifying idea is that you rarely solve an LLM problem by "prompting harder." Instead you select a pattern that matches the failure mode: missing knowledge -> RAG; weak multi-step reasoning -> chain-of-thought or tree-of-thoughts; hallucination from no world access -> ReAct with tools; cost/latency pressure -> caching, quantization, pruning, model routing; manipulation risk -> adversarial training and guardrails.

## Core concepts
- **Prompt vs. context.** A prompt is the instruction; context is the supporting information, role, examples, and retrieved knowledge assembled around it. Most robustness comes from engineering the context, not rewording the instruction.
- **In-context learning.** Zero-shot (instruction only), few-shot (examples in the prompt), and chain-of-thought (examples that demonstrate reasoning). These adapt behavior without changing weights.
- **Reasoning vs. acting.** Pure reasoning traces (CoT) improve internal logic but cannot access fresh facts; acting patterns (ReAct) let the model call tools and incorporate observations, reducing hallucination.
- **Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).** Ground responses in trusted external knowledge retrieved at inference time, overcoming the static knowledge cutoff and enterprise-data gap.
- **Embeddings and similarity.** Dense vector representations of text where semantic closeness is measured by cosine similarity; the basis for semantic retrieval.
- **Model efficiency.** Fine-tuning (adapt weights), pruning (remove low-magnitude weights/neurons), quantization (lower numeric precision, e.g., INT8), and caching trade accuracy/effort for cost, memory, and latency.
- **Adversarial robustness.** LLMs can be misled by character/word/sentence-level perturbations and universal adversarial triggers; robustness requires deliberate defense.
- **Agentic systems.** LLM-driven software that sets goals, plans, maintains memory/state, selects actions, and adapts, operating with degrees of autonomy.

## Frameworks & models
- **The five-arc lifecycle (Huang).** Data preparation (cleaning, augmentation, large-dataset handling, versioning, annotation); training/optimization (training pipeline, hyperparameter tuning, regularization, checkpointing, fine-tuning, pruning, quantization); evaluation/interpretation (metrics, cross-validation, interpretability, fairness/bias, adversarial robustness, RLHF); advanced prompting (CoT, ToT, ReAct, ReWOO, reflection, automatic multi-step reasoning/tool use); retrieval/knowledge integration (RAG, graph RAG, advanced RAG, RAG evaluation, agentic patterns).
- **ReAct (Reasoning + Acting).** An iterative Thought -> Action -> Observation loop: the model reasons in text, emits a tool action, receives an observation, and adjusts. Excels when a task needs information beyond pretraining, interaction with an environment, external tools, or stepwise decisions.
- **ReWOO (Reasoning WithOut Observation).** Decouples planning from execution: the model produces a full plan of tool calls up front rather than interleaving observations, reducing token usage and redundant LLM calls for multi-step retrieval and autonomous agents.
- **Reflection.** The model critiques and revises its own output (self-evaluation), improving error correction and reliability at the cost of extra calls.
- **RAG retrieval/generation split.** A RAG system's quality is the product of retrieval quality and generation quality; evaluation must assess both, because strong generation cannot compensate for poor retrieval, and vice versa.

## Patterns

- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting** - Intent: elicit multi-step reasoning by asking the model to show intermediate steps ("let's think step by step") or by demonstrating reasoning chains. When to use: arithmetic, logical, commonsense, and symbolic multi-step problems where a direct answer skips validating intermediate steps. Trade-offs: higher token usage and latency; error propagation across steps; quality depends on the initial prompt; not suited to non-sequential problems. Variants: few-shot CoT, dynamic CoT (generate one step at a time), adaptive CoT (route by assessed complexity).
- **Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT) prompting** - Intent: treat reasoning as a tree search, generating and evaluating multiple candidate "thoughts" at each step with branching, pruning, and backtracking. When to use: problems needing exploration of alternative paths and reconsideration of earlier steps (planning, creative/constraint problems) where CoT's single linear path is insufficient. Trade-offs: substantial overhead from generating and scoring many branches; overkill for straightforward tasks. Start with CoT and escalate to ToT only when needed.
- **ReAct (Reasoning and Acting)** - Intent: interleave verbal reasoning with tool actions and observations so the model can gather information and act, not just reason. When to use: multi-hop QA, fact verification, environment interaction, tool use (search, calculators, APIs), and tasks requiring decisions on intermediate results. Trade-offs: more LLM calls and orchestration; reliability depends on a well-constructed agent prompt template; risk of invalid actions or loops if tools/prompt are poorly specified.
- **ReWOO (Reasoning WithOut Observation)** - Intent: generate the full reasoning/tool-call plan in advance and execute it, separating planning from observation. When to use: control-flow-heavy retrieval, autonomous agents that must revisit plans, and cost-sensitive multi-step workflows where interleaved observation is wasteful. Trade-offs: less adaptive than ReAct when observations should change the plan; planning errors are costlier since they propagate to the whole execution.
- **Reflection** - Intent: have the model (or an evaluator) critique its output and regenerate to correct errors. When to use: self-improvement, error correction, and quality-critical generations. Trade-offs: extra latency/cost per reflection cycle; diminishing returns and possible over-correction.
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** - Intent: ground generation in trusted external documents retrieved (typically by embedding similarity) and injected into the prompt context. When to use: enterprise/confidential knowledge, facts past the model's cutoff, and any case where grounded, citable answers matter. Trade-offs: adds retrieval infrastructure (embeddings, vector store) and latency; quality is bounded by retrieval quality; irrelevant retrieval can mislead generation.
- **Graph-based RAG** - Intent: retrieve over a knowledge graph so the model can reason over entities and their relationships, then format the subgraph into context. When to use: QA over knowledge graphs, recommendations with explanations, scientific/biomedical and social-network reasoning where relationships matter. Trade-offs: graph traversal and embedding retrieval are expensive at scale; large subgraphs blow the context window and add noise. Mitigations: subgraph sampling, graph databases (Neo4j, Neptune), approximate nearest-neighbor search, graph summarization, context distillation.
- **Advanced RAG (multi-step/iterative/adaptive)** - Intent: go beyond single-shot retrieval with iterative retrieval, dynamic query refinement, adaptive retrieval by context/task, and meta-learning. When to use: complex QA requiring synthesis across sources, knowledge-intensive conversations, research/exploration, and ambiguous queries needing clarification. Trade-offs: each iteration adds an inference + retrieval round, making the system slow and costly; needs stopping criteria and parallelization to stay practical.
- **RAG evaluation** - Intent: assess retrieval quality and generation quality jointly, with context-sensitive metrics, end-to-end evaluation, and human judgment. When to use: any production RAG system. Trade-offs: automated IR/MT metrics (precision/recall, BLEU/ROUGE) miss usefulness and factual grounding; human evaluation is costly; root-causing whether retrieval or generation failed is hard.
- **Fine-tuning** - Intent: adapt a pretrained model's weights to a domain or task with curated examples. When to use: when in-context learning is insufficient, you need consistent domain style/behavior, and you have enough labeled data. Trade-offs: data and compute cost; risk of catastrophic forgetting; choose fine-tuning vs. RAG vs. prompting by whether the gap is knowledge (RAG), behavior/style (fine-tune), or simple instruction (prompt).
- **Model pruning** - Intent: remove low-magnitude weights, neurons, or layers (unstructured/structured, iterative, one-shot) to shrink the model. When to use: latency/memory-constrained deployment. Trade-offs: aggressive pruning degrades accuracy; usually paired with fine-tuning to recover performance, and with quantization for compounding savings.
- **Quantization** - Intent: reduce numeric precision of weights/activations (dynamic, static-with-calibration, quantization-aware training, mixed precision). When to use: cut model size and inference cost, enable edge/on-device deployment. Trade-offs: precision loss can reduce accuracy; QAT recovers more accuracy but adds training complexity; static quantization needs representative calibration data.
- **Adversarial robustness** - Intent: defend against textual adversarial attacks (character-, word-, sentence-level, and universal triggers) via adversarial training and robustness evaluation. When to use: any model exposed to untrusted input, especially classification and safety-critical generation. Trade-offs: adversarial training can slightly reduce clean accuracy and adds cost; defenses are not foolproof against novel attacks.
- **Agentic pattern (goal-directed LLM agent)** - Intent: build autonomous agents with goal-setting/planning (including hierarchical goals), memory/state management, decision-making/action selection, and learning/adaptation, with explicit safety measures. When to use: tasks too complex for a single call that benefit from autonomy, tool use, and multi-step orchestration. Trade-offs: nondeterminism and hallucination undermine full autonomy; needs guardrails, observation grounding, and human oversight; planning works in simple cases but degrades on hard ones.

## Principles & best practices
- Match the pattern to the failure mode: knowledge gap -> RAG; weak reasoning -> CoT/ToT; hallucination from no world access -> ReAct/tools; cost/latency -> caching/quantization/pruning/routing; manipulation -> adversarial defense + guardrails.
- Prefer the simplest pattern that works, and escalate (zero-shot -> few-shot -> CoT -> ToT; basic RAG -> advanced/graph RAG) only when the simpler approach is demonstrably insufficient.
- For RAG, invest in retrieval quality first; generation cannot fix bad retrieval. Tune chunking, embedding model choice, and similarity thresholds for the domain.
- Combine efficiency techniques: prune then quantize then (optionally) distill to a smaller student, and fine-tune to recover lost accuracy.
- Evaluate reasoning and final answers separately (CoT outputs), and retrieval and generation separately (RAG); use human evaluation for qualitative dimensions automated metrics miss.
- Set temperature to zero, fix seeds, and cache to reduce nondeterminism when consistency matters.
- Ground agents with observations and bound their autonomy with stopping criteria, memory limits, and safety checks.

## Anti-patterns & pitfalls
- "Prompting harder" instead of selecting the right pattern (e.g., expecting a model to answer post-cutoff facts without RAG).
- Using CoT/ToT for problems where the failure is a data gap, not a reasoning gap; the extra steps just propagate the error.
- Unbounded iterative RAG or agent loops with no cost/time budget or completion check.
- Treating high embedding similarity as relevance; similar chunks are not always the chunks that answer the question.
- Over-stuffing graph/retrieved context until it exceeds the context window or dilutes the signal with noise.
- Aggressive pruning/quantization without measuring accuracy loss or recovering via fine-tuning.
- Deploying agents or tool-calling without guardrails against prompt injection and invalid actions.

## Decision guidance
- **Prompting vs. RAG vs. fine-tuning.** Simple/known task -> prompt (zero/few-shot). Missing or fast-changing knowledge -> RAG. Consistent domain behavior/style with enough data -> fine-tune. These compose (RAG + fine-tuned reasoning + CoT).
- **CoT vs. ToT.** Default to CoT; move to ToT only when the problem needs exploration/backtracking and CoT's single path fails.
- **ReAct vs. ReWOO.** ReAct when observations should steer reasoning step by step; ReWOO when you can plan tool calls up front and want lower token cost.
- **Basic vs. advanced/graph RAG.** Start basic; add re-ranking/iteration (advanced) when single-shot retrieval misses, and graph RAG when relationships between entities carry the answer.
- **Efficiency stack.** Latency/memory pressure -> quantize first (cheap, broad), then prune, then distill; fine-tune to recover accuracy. Repetitive traffic -> cache before scaling hardware.
- **Safety.** Any untrusted input path needs adversarial-robustness evaluation and guardrails; agentic/tool-calling paths additionally need prompt-injection defenses.

## Recent developments (2024-2026)

- **Reasoning became a first-class, trainable model capability rather than only a prompting pattern.** OpenAI's o-series (o3/o3-mini, early 2025) and GPT-5 (August 2025, unifying the reasoning and generalist tracks behind a "thinking-budget" API parameter), Gemini 3 Pro, Claude Opus 4.5, and open-weight DeepSeek-R1/V3.2 internalized chain-of-thought via RL. CoT/ToT prompting patterns still apply, but increasingly the model decides reasoning depth itself (Vellum / vendor releases, 2025-2026).
- **RAG evolved into agentic and graph variants.** "Agentic RAG" (agents plan multi-step retrieval, reflect on intermediate answers, and adapt) and Microsoft's GraphRAG (entity-relation graphs for global/query-focused summarization) became mainstream paradigms in 2024-2025, extending this file's basic and graph-RAG patterns with planning and tool use (IBM / survey literature, 2025).
- **Quantization advanced toward near-lossless low precision.** 4-bit and lower (GPTQ, AWQ, GGUF, plus FP8/FP4 hardware support) and long-context efficiency methods (e.g., DeepSeek Sparse Attention reducing complexity to near-linear) made the pruning/quantization/efficiency patterns here far more practical for production and edge deployment (DeepSeek / ecosystem, 2025).
- **Prompt injection moved from a robustness footnote to the central agentic threat.** Simon Willison's "lethal trifecta" (June 2025) and confirmed indirect-injection exploits made the adversarial-robustness and guardrails patterns load-bearing for any tool-calling or RAG path, reinforcing this file's "any untrusted input needs adversarial-robustness evaluation and guardrails" guidance (Simon Willison / HiddenLayer, 2025-2026).
- **Tool calling standardized on MCP.** The ReAct/tool-use patterns here are now commonly implemented via the Model Context Protocol, which became a Linux Foundation standard (December 2025) with an official registry, decoupling tool hosting from the agent (modelcontextprotocol.io, 2025).
- **LLM-as-judge and trajectory evaluation got standardized observability.** OpenTelemetry GenAI Semantic Conventions, LangSmith, and Langfuse standardized how RAG and reasoning outputs are traced and scored, addressing this file's "evaluate retrieval and generation separately" guidance with tooling (OpenTelemetry / Langfuse, 2025-2026).
- **Context engineering reframed "engineer the context, not the instruction."** Anthropic (September 2025) and LangChain (July 2025) named context engineering and added concrete techniques (context editing/pruning, just-in-time retrieval, file-based memory tools), directly extending this file's prompt-vs-context distinction (Anthropic / LangChain, 2025).
